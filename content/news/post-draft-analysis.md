The draft is over, the stickers are on the board, and—by a remarkable coincidence—every manager believes they won. Some see a title team. Others see “upside.” Nobody sees the waiver claims they will be filing in three weeks.

Now we have the first ruling from the cold, humorless machine. The site's [preseason roster evaluation](/preseason) uses full-season ESPN projections, legal optimized lineups, positional value over replacement, and useful depth. It does not care who had the best draft-room speech or whose sleeper received the loudest groan. For the full explanation, take it up with the [methodology](/methodology).

The verdict is not that one roster is flawless. It is that some teams left the draft with several ways to win, while others are asking one position room to cover a suspicious number of holes.

## The Post-Draft Power Rankings

**[Alls fair in Olave and Warren](/teams/9)** opens at No. 1 because its roster has the best blend of premium starters and few genuine emergencies. Christian McCaffrey and Chase Brown form the league's second-ranked RB room, Jalen Hurts supplies a top-three QB room, and Rashee Rice is a real FLEX weapon rather than a lineup obligation. The model gives this team the strongest overall roster even with only the fifth-ranked receivers.

The first chase group is far less conventional. **[Netra's Nasty Team](/teams/8)** sits second behind the league's best receivers and second-ranked FLEX. **[Rahul](/teams/6)** follows at No. 3 behind the best RB room and the second-best TE room. **[teenage mutant njigba turtles](/teams/7)** is fourth, pairing the league's best QB and TE rooms—Josh Allen and Trey McBride—with Bijan Robinson, but carrying far less useful depth than the teams above it.

The middle starts with **[The Nabers Have Complained](/teams/2)** at No. 5, a team with terrifying receivers and more RB depth than its ninth-place RB score initially implies. **[Akash's Astounding Team](/teams/4)** is sixth, followed by **[mein kraft](/teams/10)** in seventh and **[Olaf's Otherworldly Team](/teams/1)** in eighth. All three have obvious winning combinations. All three also have a room the model would prefer to inspect with protective gloves.

At the bottom, **[Jayashree's Scary Team](/teams/3)** lands ninth despite a collection of imposing starters, and **[step-burrow i'm stuck!](/teams/5)** starts tenth. Those are not death sentences. They are, however, strongly worded invitations to prove the projections wrong.

## Who We're High On

### Alls fair in Olave and Warren

McCaffrey is the centerpiece, projecting for 342.8 points and carrying nearly 140 points of value over the RB replacement line by himself. Brown makes the room more than a one-man show, while Hurts, Tyler Warren, Rice, Chris Olave, and Davante Adams create a starting lineup without an easy target. Matthew Stafford is also the most valuable backup quarterback in this league's depth model. The bench is not spectacular across the board, but the starting structure is the cleanest in the field.

### Netra's Nasty Team

No roster can match Puka Nacua and Drake London at receiver. Nacua's 356.3-point projection is the engine, London adds another 269.8, and Jameson Williams gives the room the only top-rated WR depth score. Then Jeremiyah Love slides into the FLEX with 278.5 projected points. The RB and TE rooms keep Netra out of the top spot, but if Travis Kelce beats a modest projection, this roster can make the model look conservative very quickly.

### The Nabers Have Complained

Justin Jefferson and CeeDee Lamb are both projected just under 294 points. Malik Nabers is the FLEX. That is an absurd weekly receiving foundation before Jayden Daniels even enters the conversation. The model also ranks this bench first because D'Andre Swift remains meaningfully above the RB replacement line after the starters are filled. The ninth-ranked RB room and ordinary TE situation are real concerns, but no opponent will enjoy trying to match this team's three-receiver ceiling.

## Who We're Low On

### step-burrow i'm stuck!

De'Von Achane and James Cook III give the last-place roster the league's third-best RB room, so this is not a talent-free operation. The problem is everything the backs are being asked to disguise. The WR room ranks tenth, Dak Prescott does not clear the QB replacement line in this model, Kyle Pitts leads the eighth-ranked TE room, and no bench player contributes positive value over replacement. Achane and Cook may have to drag this roster uphill every Sunday.

### Olaf's Otherworldly Team

Amon-Ra St. Brown and Garrett Wilson power the No. 3 receiver room, with Saquon Barkley giving the roster another elite name. After that, the construction gets uncomfortable. Justin Herbert anchors the league's lowest-rated QB room, Bucky Irving produces the ninth-ranked FLEX result, and the bench supplies no positive value over replacement. Eighth overall looks excellent on a poster. The model is less impressed by what happens when the poster needs a substitute.

### Jayashree's Scary Team

Jonathan Taylor, Drake Maye, Nico Collins, A.J. Brown, and Colston Loveland give Jayashree legitimate weekly punch. The issue is that the roster repeatedly falls from “star” to “question” without much cushioning. The WR room ranks eighth, the bench ranks sixth but produces no positive replacement value, and DK Metcalf's current full-season projection sits below the WR replacement line. There is upside here, but not much margin for error.

## Fraud Watch

**teenage mutant njigba turtles**, please report to the inspection desk. Josh Allen, Bijan Robinson, and Trey McBride make this roster look like it should be printed on championship merchandise. Underneath those names: the ninth-ranked WR room, the eighth-ranked FLEX, and the tenth-ranked bench. Caleb Williams, Tony Pollard, Matthew Golden, and Stefon Diggs are recognizable depth, but none currently clears the model's position-specific replacement threshold. This fourth-ranked team can absolutely win the league. It can also discover that three superstars are not a contingency plan.

**Rahul** also gets a provisional fraud stamp—not because No. 3 is indefensible, but because the roster is dramatically top-heavy by position. Jahmyr Gibbs and Ashton Jeanty own the best RB room, and Brock Bowers nearly gives Rahul the best TE room too. Yet the FLEX ranks tenth, the bench eighth, and the QB room seventh. Patrick Mahomes is on the bench, but his current full-season projection remains just below this league's QB replacement line. If the elite rooms merely perform well instead of detonating, the support structure has work to do.

## Draft Winners

**Alls fair in Olave and Warren** won by building the best complete starting lineup, not simply collecting the loudest names. It finished top three at QB, RB, and TE, then added a top-four FLEX.

**Netra's Nasty Team** won by monopolizing receiver firepower. Nacua and London are the best starting WR pair in the model, while Love gives the lineup a third high-end skill-position hammer. That is how a fourth-ranked team ends up looking like a plausible favorite.

**teenage mutant njigba turtles** won the stars-and-scrubs version of the exercise. Allen, Robinson, and McBride create three separate positional advantages. The depth critique is coming—and has arrived—but landing the No. 1 QB and TE rooms in the same draft deserves credit.

## Draft Losers

**step-burrow i'm stuck!** drafted two excellent running backs and then left too many replacement-level or worse outcomes around them. The model sees the lowest overall roster, worst receiver room, and no useful bench surplus.

**Olaf's Otherworldly Team** acquired premium receiver names but did not build enough beneath them. Ninth overall, last at QB, ninth at FLEX, and no positive bench VOR is a precarious combination.

**Jayashree's Scary Team** has enough star power to object loudly to this label. The objection is noted. The response is that ninth overall, eighth at WR, and a zero-positive-VOR bench is not the post-draft profile of a team that finished the job.

## Best Position Rooms

- **Best QB Room — teenage mutant njigba turtles.** Josh Allen's 369.7-point season projection is the highest among the league's rostered quarterbacks and drives the clear No. 1 room.
- **Best RB Room — Rahul.** Jahmyr Gibbs and Ashton Jeanty combine elite top-end value; Gibbs alone sits roughly 162 points above the RB replacement level.
- **Best WR Room — Netra's Nasty Team.** Puka Nacua and Drake London supply the best starting score, and Jameson Williams makes this the deepest positively valued WR room too.
- **Best TE Room — teenage mutant njigba turtles.** Trey McBride narrowly beats Brock Bowers in the model and gives the No. 2 overall roster another premium edge.
- **Best FLEX — mein kraft.** Ja'Marr Chase projects for 337.5 points and creates more than 133 points of value above the WR replacement line. Calling him a FLEX feels almost disrespectful.
- **Best Bench — The Nabers Have Complained.** D'Andre Swift is the league's strongest useful reserve after optimized starters are assigned. The label reflects positive value over replacement, not the number of famous names on a bench.

## Biggest Strengths

The best rooms are not the only dangerous ones. **Alls fair in Olave and Warren** trails only Rahul at RB, and McCaffrey gives it the most explosive single RB advantage outside Gibbs. **The Nabers Have Complained** is second at WR, but Jefferson, Lamb, and Nabers may be the league's most intimidating three-man receiver deployment. **Olaf's Otherworldly Team** is third at WR because St. Brown and Wilson can carry enormous volume. **Akash's Astounding Team** owns the second-ranked QB room with Lamar Jackson, the fourth-ranked WR room behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba, and the third-best FLEX with Josh Jacobs. Sixth overall has teeth.

And then there is **mein kraft**, a seventh-ranked roster with the league's best FLEX. Chase can erase plenty of structural mistakes by himself, while Kenneth Walker III and Omarion Hampton give the RB room a solid sixth-place base. 

## Biggest Weaknesses

**Akash's Astounding Team** has the lowest-rated RB room despite Derrick Henry and Josh Jacobs being on the roster. The optimizer needs Jacobs at FLEX, leaving David Montgomery below the RB replacement line as the second required starter. 

**mein kraft** has the league's weakest TE room with Dallas Goedert providing no value above replacement. **step-burrow i'm stuck!** owns the weakest WR room; Jaylen Waddle and DJ Moore combine for only a small surplus over the line. **Rahul's** tenth-ranked FLEX asks Luther Burden III to do a job for which the current projection offers almost no cushion. And the glamorous **teenage mutant njigba turtles** bench is last because every reserve currently sits at or below replacement value.

## Sleeper Team

**Akash's Astounding Team** is the pick. The sixth-place ranking is being dragged down by the RB configuration, but Lamar Jackson, Smith-Njigba, George Pickens, Josh Jacobs, and a top-three bench score provide multiple paths upward. Tucker Kraft is a positively valued TE reserve, and the Rams defense also grades well. Fix the second RB spot—or watch Montgomery beat the projection—and this roster can jump a tier without needing a miracle.

## Most Fragile Contender

It has to be **teenage mutant njigba turtles**. No. 2 overall is a contender by definition, and Allen, Robinson, and McBride give it a championship ceiling. But the model finds no positive bench VOR, while the starting WR and FLEX groups already sit in the bottom three. One injury to a centerpiece would force the roster to lean on exactly the depth the rankings distrust most.

## Bold Predictions

1. **Netra's Nasty Team converts its No. 2 preseason rank into a league title** behind the league's best receiver room.
2. **The Nabers Have Complained produces the league's most feared weekly receiver trio**, even if its RB room prevents a top-two finish.
3. **Akash's Astounding Team cracks the top four** after solving—or simply surviving—the second RB spot.
4. **teenage mutant njigba turtles finishes outside the top five** because its depth becomes a real problem at least once.
5. **step-burrow i'm stuck! wins a week on Achane and Cook alone**, but the WR room keeps the team in the bottom half over the full season.

## Final Verdict

**Alls fair in Olave and Warren** enters Week 1 as the team to beat: the best overall construction, an elite RB foundation, and no glaring starting-lineup collapse point. **teenage mutant njigba turtles**, **Rahul**, and **Netra's Nasty Team** are the immediate challengers, each with a sharper positional superpower and a more obvious place to attack.

The middle is volatile enough to ruin this entire article by October. The Nabers Have Complained can overwhelm teams through the air. Akash can rise quickly. mein kraft has Chase. Jayashree has stars. Olaf has a championship-caliber receiver duo. step-burrow has two backs capable of turning any matchup.

Everyone got their draft grade. Now comes the inconvenient part: Week 1 gets a vote.
